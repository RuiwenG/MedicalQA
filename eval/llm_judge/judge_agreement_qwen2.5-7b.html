Qwen2.5-7B Judge Agreement
MedicalQA · LLM-as-judge

Three judges on 678 Qwen2.5-7B Q&A pairs

The SingleAgent pairs were regenerated with Qwen2.5-7B, a small generator, from the v1, v2 and v3 prompts. DeepSeek, gpt-oss and Gemma 4 scored every pair on the same five-metric rubric: gpt-oss and Gemma three times each, DeepSeek twice so far. This page compares the three prompt versions as each judge sees them, and how far the judges agree.

Findings

v1 ≥ v3

The Standalone rule does not help this model

Standalone pass rate, v1 / v2 / v3: DeepSeek 87.4 / 81.9 / 84.3%, Gemma 83.3 / 79.4 / 82.6%, gpt-oss 97.2 / 95.1 / 95.9%. All three judges rate v2 below v1, with no overlap between the run ranges, and none rates v3 above v1. Qwen2.5-7B does not turn the self-containment instruction into more self-contained pairs.

up to 12%

Ratings now reach the bottom of the scale

Gemma puts 10–12% of pairs at the lowest level on Trustworthiness, Usefulness, Standalone and the recommendation. DeepSeek recommends only 58–60% of pairs without edits and says 12–17% need major edits. Clarity is still concentrated: Gemma rates 95–96% of pairs at the top level.

Fleiss κ 0.40–0.46

More to agree on, more agreement

On pass/fail, the three judges agree at Fleiss κ 0.42 on Trustworthiness, 0.40 on Usefulness and 0.46 on Standalone. DeepSeek and Gemma agree on Standalone at κ 0.74, below DeepSeek's agreement with its own repeat run (0.86).

83–85%

gpt-oss is the lenient judge

gpt-oss recommends 83–85% of pairs without edits, against 58–64% for the other two, and passes 95–97% on Standalone where they pass 79–87%. Its Standalone agreement with either other judge is κ 0.27–0.28.

Similarity

How often they agree

One matrix per metric. Outlined diagonal cells compare a judge with its own repeat runs, which is the most two raters could be expected to reach. The other cells compare two different judges. Darker means more agreement; pick the statistic above the matrices.

Raw agreement is high on every metric because most pairs pass. Cohen's κ removes the agreement two raters would reach by chance. On Clarity, Usefulness and Care Safety nearly every pair passes, so κ falls toward zero even where judges almost never differ, and can be undefined when a judge passes every pair in every run. Gwet's AC1 holds up at those base rates. Trustworthiness and Standalone have enough failures for κ to be informative.

Multi-rater agreement

All judges at once: Fleiss' κ

Fleiss' κ extends Cohen's κ to any number of raters, so it gives one chance-corrected figure for all judges together instead of one per pair. It is read on the same scale: 0 is chance, 1 is perfect. It treats the 4-level rating as plain categories, so a one-level miss counts as a full disagreement, which keeps it below weighted κ. The same base-rate caution applies as for Cohen's κ.

"All judges, one run each" takes one run from every judge and computes Fleiss' κ across them, repeated for every combination of runs; the range beneath is the lowest and highest combination. "Consensus" uses each judge's majority verdict or median rating. "Every run" treats all runs from all judges as separate raters. "Within" columns treat a judge's own runs as separate raters, which measures that judge's consistency.

Why κ is low when the judges agree

High agreement, low Fleiss' κ

κ does not ask how often raters agree, but how much better than chance: κ = (observed − chance) / (1 − chance). Chance is estimated from how often each rating is used, so when most ratings share one level, chance agreement is already close to observed agreement and κ stays low even when the judges match. Gwet's AC1 uses a chance term that stays small in that situation; quadratic-weighted κ gives partial credit for one-level misses.

What drives it on this set

  1. Concentration at the top. Clarity pairs match 70% of the time against 68% by chance, so Fleiss' κ is 0.07 while AC1 is 0.66. Standalone matches 83% against 76%: κ 0.29, AC1 0.82.
  2. Different strictness. On Usefulness DeepSeek gives the top level to 14% of v3 pairs, gpt-oss to 68% and Gemma to 40%. The judges are within one level on 87% of pairs but give the same level on only 29%, which is why Usefulness has the lowest raw agreement and AC1, while its quadratic κ (0.41) is among the highest.
  3. Real disagreement on Trustworthiness. Trustworthiness has the most room above chance (73% vs 62%) and the highest quadratic κ (0.47), yet all three judges are within one level on only 82% of pairs: in the first run of each judge, 48 pairs are rated Excellent by one judge and Poor by another.

Method

How this was measured

Generator
qwen/qwen-2.5-7b-instruct through OpenRouter, pinned to Phala, its only provider (precision not published), fallbacks disabled. The model has no reasoning mode. Seed 42, temperature 0.3, top-p 0.9; Phala applies the repetition penalty of 1.05 that other providers ignore. All 25 videos per prompt. The prompts are the v1, v2 and v3 SingleAgent prompts from git with one format line changed, as for the Qwen3-14B set: with the bare labels ("Question 1:") this model wrote the timestamp in place of the question in 22 v1 and 27 v2 pairs, so the line shows placeholders ("Question 1: <question text>"). The quality criteria are unchanged.
Same pairs, same rubric
All 678 pairs were judged by every model on identical text, checked by question text across all 8 runs. All used the five-metric rubric with the team's Trustworthiness definition, the transcript in the prompt, and temperature 0.
DeepSeek
deepseek-v4-pro through the DeepSeek API, reasoning by default. 2 runs on 3 October 2026; a third waits on API credit.
gpt-oss
openai/gpt-oss-120b through OpenRouter, restricted to bf16 providers with fallbacks disabled. Reasoning is always on for this model; it ran at its default medium effort. Three runs on 3 October 2026.
Gemma 4
google/gemma-4-31b-it through OpenRouter, pinned to one provider (DeepInfra) at fp8, fallbacks disabled. Only two providers served it at bf16 with JSON output, and when one degraded the other could not sustain a full run, so Gemma ran at fp8 while gpt-oss ran at bf16. Reasoning is off by default for this model and was switched on. Three runs on 3 October 2026.
Independent runs
The gpt-oss and Gemma runs paused when the OpenRouter key reached its spending cap and resumed from their own saved results; one DeepSeek reply that was not valid JSON and a few Gemma pairs were re-judged inside their own runs. No run mixes in another run's ratings.
Consensus
Majority of a judge's runs for a Yes/No verdict, median for a 4-level rating. With DeepSeek's two runs a tied verdict counts as a pass and the median is the better rating.
Raw agreement
Share of pairs where both raters gave the same verdict.
Cohen's κ
Agreement beyond what two raters with the same pass rates would reach by chance. 0 is chance level, 1 is perfect.
Gwet's AC1
A chance-corrected agreement that stays stable when nearly every pair falls in one category, where κ becomes misleading.
Weighted κ and Spearman ρ
For the 4-level ratings. Weighted κ counts a one-level miss as a small disagreement and a three-level miss as a large one. Spearman ρ measures whether two raters order pairs the same way.
Exact and within one level
Share of pairs with the same 4-level rating, and with ratings at most one level apart.
