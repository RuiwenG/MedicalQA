MedicalQA Judge Agreement
MedicalQA · LLM-as-judge

Three judges on the same 677 Q&A pairs

DeepSeek, gpt-oss and Gemma 4 each scored the same SingleAgent Q&A pairs, from the v1, v2 and v3 generation prompts, three times on the same five-metric rubric. This page compares every rating they produced.

Pairs 677 (v1 226 · v2 225 · v3 226) Runs 3 per judge Pair-judgments 6,093 Rubric 5 metrics + recommendation

Findings

κ 0.85

Gemma 4 reproduces DeepSeek on Standalone

Both rank the prompts v1 < v2 < v3 with nearly the same pass rates (Gemma 81.6 / 91.6 / 98.7%, DeepSeek 79.9 / 91.4 / 98.1%) and the same size of effect, +17.1 and +18.1 points. Their agreement on individual pairs, κ 0.85, matches DeepSeek's agreement with its own repeat runs (0.84).

94.8%

gpt-oss is the outlier

It passes 94.8% of v1 pairs on Standalone, where the other two pass about 80%, so it registers a v1→v3 gain of only 4.6 points. Its agreement with either other judge is κ 0.32–0.35. Gemma fails 51 pairs gpt-oss passes; the reverse happens twice.

Fleiss κ 0.23

No judge pair agrees on Trustworthiness

Agreement between any two judges is κ 0.15–0.34, against 0.57–0.77 for each judge with itself. DeepSeek fails 47 pairs, gpt-oss 25 and Gemma 20, mostly over hallucination flags the others don't raise. Hallucination rates from any one judge should be read with that in mind.

3 of 3

Every judge prefers v3

The share of pairs recommended to caregivers without edits rises from v1 to v3 for all three judges: DeepSeek 71→86%, gpt-oss 91→96%, Gemma 79→96%. No judge finds a cost to the self-containment rule on Clarity, Usefulness or Care Safety.

Similarity

How often they agree

One matrix per metric. Outlined diagonal cells compare a judge with its own repeat runs, which is the most two raters could be expected to reach. The other cells compare two different judges. Darker means more agreement; pick the statistic above the matrices.

Raw agreement is high on every metric because most pairs pass. Cohen's κ removes the agreement two raters would reach by chance. On Clarity, Usefulness and Care Safety nearly every pair passes, so κ falls toward zero even where judges almost never differ, and can be undefined when a judge passes every pair in every run. Gwet's AC1 holds up at those base rates. Trustworthiness and Standalone have enough failures for κ to be informative.

Multi-rater agreement

All judges at once: Fleiss' κ

Fleiss' κ extends Cohen's κ to any number of raters, so it gives one chance-corrected figure for all judges together instead of one per pair. It is read on the same scale: 0 is chance, 1 is perfect. It treats the 4-level rating as plain categories, so a one-level miss counts as a full disagreement, which keeps it below weighted κ. The same base-rate caution applies as for Cohen's κ.

"All judges, one run each" takes one run from every judge and computes Fleiss' κ across them, repeated for every combination of runs; the range beneath is the lowest and highest combination. "Consensus" uses each judge's majority verdict or median rating. "Every run" treats all runs from all judges as separate raters. "Within" columns treat a judge's own three runs as three raters, which measures that judge's consistency.

Why κ is low when the judges agree

High agreement, low Fleiss' κ

The judges give the same rating most of the time, but Fleiss' κ stays between 0 and 0.4. They really do agree; the low κ comes mostly from how κ treats skewed ratings (the "kappa paradox"). κ does not ask how often raters agree. It asks how much better than chance they agree: κ = (observed − chance) / (1 − chance). Chance is estimated from how often each rating is used overall, so when nearly every Q&A gets the top rating, chance agreement is already close to observed agreement and there is almost nothing left to beat.

"Judges agree" is the share of judge pairs giving the same rating to a Q&A, as used inside Fleiss' κ; "Chance agrees" is the agreement expected if each rating were drawn at random with the observed overall frequencies. Each figure is the mean over all 27 combinations of one run per judge, with the lowest and highest combination beneath when they differ. Gwet AC1 uses the same observed agreement but a chance term that stays small when ratings pile into one level (4 levels per metric). Quadratic κ is the mean of the three pairwise Cohen's κ with quadratic weights, so a one-level miss costs 1/9 of a three-level miss.

Three reasons the number is low

  1. The ratings pile up at the top of the scale. On v3, Standalone pairs match 98% of the time, and random labelling with the same frequencies would match 97%. κ is then computed from the handful of borderline items, which is why it is 0.26 despite near-total agreement. Trustworthiness (85% vs 84%) and the recommendation (88% vs 86%) behave the same way.
  2. The judges use the scale differently. DeepSeek is the strictest and often places a Q&A one level down ("Easy" rather than "Very easy", "Useful" rather than "Highly useful"); Gemma 4 almost never leaves the top level (table below). They agree on whether a Q&A is good, not on whether it is top-tier. Clarity shows this most: all three are within one level on 98% of v3 Q&As, yet Fleiss' κ is 0.00.
  3. Fleiss' κ, as computed here, ignores how far apart two ratings are. It is the nominal form, used to match the human-annotator figure, so "Excellent" vs "Good" counts as a full disagreement, the same as "Excellent" vs "Poor". Almost all judge disagreements are one level apart.

How the other two coefficients respond

Gwet AC1 was designed for exactly this situation: its chance term shrinks when ratings concentrate in one level. On v3 it sits at 0.65–0.98, close to the raw agreement, and it is the figure to quote alongside raw and within-one agreement. Quadratic-weighted κ gives partial credit for near misses but is still corrected with the same rating frequencies, so it rises only modestly (0.06–0.29 on v3). It is also pulled down by DeepSeek's systematic one-level offset: a constant strictness difference counts against weighted κ even when the two judges rank the Q&As in the same order. So weighted κ does not fix the paradox here; AC1 and the raw agreement figures describe the agreement better.

The same explanation fits the human annotators' pooled Fleiss' κ of 0.088. The one genuine disagreement is Trustworthiness: only 91% of v3 Q&As have all three judges within one level, because DeepSeek rates about one in ten "Fair" where the other two say "Excellent".

Method

How this was measured

Same pairs, same rubric
All 677 pairs were judged by every model on identical text, checked by question text across all nine runs. All used the five-metric rubric with the team's Trustworthiness definition, the transcript in the prompt, and temperature 0.
DeepSeek
deepseek-v4-pro through the DeepSeek API, reasoning by default. Runs from 9 and 11 September 2026; run 1 combines the per-prompt runs from 9 September.
gpt-oss
openai/gpt-oss-120b through OpenRouter, restricted to bf16 providers with fallbacks disabled. Reasoning is always on for this model; it ran at its default medium effort. Three runs on 1 October 2026.
Gemma 4
google/gemma-4-31b-it through OpenRouter, pinned to one provider (DeepInfra) at fp8, fallbacks disabled. Only two providers served it at bf16 with JSON output, and when one degraded the other could not sustain a full run, so Gemma ran at fp8 while gpt-oss ran at bf16. Reasoning is off by default for this model and was switched on. Three runs on 2 October 2026.
Independent runs
Pairs lost to rate limits were re-judged inside the same run, using only that run's own results, so no run mixes in another run's ratings.
Consensus
Majority of three runs for a Yes/No verdict, median of three for a 4-level rating.
Raw agreement
Share of pairs where both raters gave the same verdict.
Cohen's κ
Agreement beyond what two raters with the same pass rates would reach by chance. 0 is chance level, 1 is perfect.
Gwet's AC1
A chance-corrected agreement that stays stable when nearly every pair falls in one category, where κ becomes misleading.
Weighted κ and Spearman ρ
For the 4-level ratings. Weighted κ counts a one-level miss as a small disagreement and a three-level miss as a large one. Spearman ρ measures whether two raters order pairs the same way.
Exact and within one level
Share of pairs with the same 4-level rating, and with ratings at most one level apart.
Computed from the judge output CSVs in eval/results/. The transcript-free judge was run only with DeepSeek and is not compared here.
