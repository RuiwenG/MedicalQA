Qwen3-14B Judge Agreement
MedicalQA · LLM-as-judge

Three judges on 678 Qwen3-14B Q&A pairs

The SingleAgent pairs were regenerated with Qwen3-14B, a smaller generator, from the v1, v2 and v3 prompts. DeepSeek, gpt-oss and Gemma 4 each scored every pair three times on the same five-metric rubric. This page compares the three prompt versions as each judge sees them, and how far the judges agree.

3 of 3

Every judge ranks the prompts v1 < v2 < v3 on Standalone

Standalone pass rate, v1 / v2 / v3: DeepSeek 92.2 / 94.1 / 97.1%, Gemma 92.3 / 93.8 / 97.9%, gpt-oss 97.1 / 98.5 / 99.4%. For each judge the v1 and v3 run ranges do not overlap.

−3.2 / −4.6 pts

v3 costs Trustworthiness for two judges

DeepSeek passes 90.3% of v1 pairs on Trustworthiness and 87.0% of v3; Gemma 93.1% and 88.5%. Neither judge's run ranges overlap between v1 and v3. gpt-oss sees no change (94.8 → 94.5%). The same two judges flag hallucination more often on v3: DeepSeek on 7.2% of v1 ratings and 10.8% of v3, Gemma 3.5% and 7.8% (gpt-oss 4.3% and 3.8%). Asking this model to name the subject in every pair seems to push it to add detail the video does not support.

κ 0.88

Gemma 4 reproduces DeepSeek on Standalone

DeepSeek and Gemma agree on Standalone pass/fail at κ 0.88, as high as DeepSeek agrees with its own repeat runs (0.87). gpt-oss passes almost everything and agrees with either at κ 0.39–0.40.

≤6%

Ratings still sit at the top of the scale

No judge puts more than 6% of any version's pairs at the lowest level of any metric. Trustworthiness from DeepSeek is the widest spread (v3: 70 / 18 / 10 / 2% across the four levels); Gemma rates every pair's Clarity at the top level. See "How the scores spread" below.

Similarity

How often they agree

One matrix per metric. Outlined diagonal cells compare a judge with its own repeat runs, which is the most two raters could be expected to reach. The other cells compare two different judges. Darker means more agreement; pick the statistic above the matrices.

Raw agreement is high on every metric because most pairs pass. Cohen's κ removes the agreement two raters would reach by chance. On Clarity, Usefulness and Care Safety nearly every pair passes, so κ falls toward zero even where judges almost never differ, and can be undefined when a judge passes every pair in every run. Gwet's AC1 holds up at those base rates. Trustworthiness and Standalone have enough failures for κ to be informative.

Multi-rater agreement

All judges at once: Fleiss' κ

Fleiss' κ extends Cohen's κ to any number of raters, so it gives one chance-corrected figure for all judges together instead of one per pair. It is read on the same scale: 0 is chance, 1 is perfect. It treats the 4-level rating as plain categories, so a one-level miss counts as a full disagreement, which keeps it below weighted κ. The same base-rate caution applies as for Cohen's κ.

"All judges, one run each" takes one run from every judge and computes Fleiss' κ across them, repeated for every combination of runs; the range beneath is the lowest and highest combination. "Consensus" uses each judge's majority verdict or median rating. "Every run" treats all runs from all judges as separate raters. "Within" columns treat a judge's own three runs as three raters, which measures that judge's consistency.

Why κ is low when the judges agree

High agreement, low Fleiss' κ

κ does not ask how often raters agree, but how much better than chance: κ = (observed − chance) / (1 − chance). Chance is estimated from how often each rating is used, so when most ratings share one level, chance agreement is already close to observed agreement and κ stays low even when the judges match. Gwet's AC1 uses a chance term that stays small in that situation; quadratic-weighted κ gives partial credit for one-level misses.

What drives it on this set

  1. Concentration at the top. Standalone pairs match 95% of the time against 92% by chance, and Clarity 89% against 88%, so κ is 0.40 and 0.07 while AC1 is 0.95 and 0.88.
  2. Different strictness. On Usefulness DeepSeek gives the top level to 23% of v3 pairs, gpt-oss to 79% and Gemma to 56%. The judges are within one level on 95% of pairs but give the same level on only 34%, which is why Usefulness has the lowest raw agreement and AC1 here.
  3. Real disagreement on Trustworthiness. Trustworthiness has the most room above chance (79% vs 73%) and the highest quadratic κ of the quality metrics (0.41), yet all three judges are within one level on only 88% of pairs: in the first run alone, 30 pairs are rated Excellent by one judge and Poor by another.

Method

How this was measured

Generator
qwen/qwen3-14b through OpenRouter, pinned to Alibaba with fallbacks disabled; Alibaba is the only provider of this model that honours reasoning off (0 reasoning tokens, verified). Seed 42, temperature 0.3, all 25 videos per prompt. The prompts are the v1, v2 and v3 SingleAgent prompts verbatim from git, except one format line: with the bare labels ("Question 1:") this model left the question out, so the line shows placeholders ("Question 1: <question text>"). The quality criteria are unchanged.
Same pairs, same rubric
All 678 pairs were judged by every model on identical text, checked by question text across all nine runs. All used the five-metric rubric with the team's Trustworthiness definition, the transcript in the prompt, and temperature 0.
DeepSeek
deepseek-v4-pro through the DeepSeek API, reasoning by default. Three runs on 2–3 October 2026.
gpt-oss
openai/gpt-oss-120b through OpenRouter, restricted to bf16 providers with fallbacks disabled. Reasoning is always on for this model; it ran at its default medium effort. Three runs on 2–3 October 2026.
Gemma 4
google/gemma-4-31b-it through OpenRouter, pinned to one provider (DeepInfra) at fp8, fallbacks disabled. Only two providers served it at bf16 with JSON output, and when one degraded the other could not sustain a full run, so Gemma ran at fp8 while gpt-oss ran at bf16. Reasoning is off by default for this model and was switched on. Three runs on 2–3 October 2026.
Independent runs
All nine runs were started together and each judged all 678 pairs on its first pass; none needed re-judging. Each run is independent of the others.
Consensus
Majority of three runs for a Yes/No verdict, median of three for a 4-level rating.
Raw agreement
Share of pairs where both raters gave the same verdict.
Cohen's κ
Agreement beyond what two raters with the same pass rates would reach by chance. 0 is chance level, 1 is perfect.
Gwet's AC1
A chance-corrected agreement that stays stable when nearly every pair falls in one category, where κ becomes misleading.
Weighted κ and Spearman ρ
For the 4-level ratings. Weighted κ counts a one-level miss as a small disagreement and a three-level miss as a large one. Spearman ρ measures whether two raters order pairs the same way.
Exact and within one level
Share of pairs with the same 4-level rating, and with ratings at most one level apart.
